Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions (MSCA) Trends

Analysis of Fellowships Data (2014 - 2026)

Data sources: EU Funding & Tenders Portal, European Commission MSCA News (5 Dec 2023), and Regulation (EU) 2021/695.

Executive Summary

This report analyzes the trends in the Marie Skłodowska-Curie Actions (MSCA) Fellowships over a 13-year period, spanning from 2014 to 2026. The data reflects a transition in the grant umbrella from MSCA-IF (Individual Fellowships) to MSCA-EF (European Fellowships) starting around 2021/2022.

Key observations indicate significant volatility in recent years, particularly concerning the volume of presented proposals and the subsequent impact on the chance of securing funding. While the overall budget has seen an upward trend recently, it has not kept pace with the explosive growth in applications.

1. Budget Allocation Trends (M€)

Analysis:

The allocated budget has experienced fluctuations. A steady increase was observed from 2014 (240.5 M€) to a peak in 2020 (328 M€) under the MSCA-IF umbrella. Following a dip in 2021-2022 during the transition period, the budget has seen a substantial increase, reaching its highest points in 2024 (417.2 M€) and stabilizing around 400 M€ for 2025 and 2026. The sharp increase in 2024 correlates with the United Kingdom officially joining the Horizon Europe programme, which expanded the total available budget pool (estimated contribution ~169M€/year, estimated 15% increase in participations).

2. Presented vs. Funded Proposals

Analysis:

This is perhaps the most worrying trend. From 2014 to 2023, the number of presented/eligible proposals hovered mostly between 7,000 and 11,500. However, starting in 2024, there has been an unprecedented increase in applications. Proposals jumped to over 10,000 in 2024, nearly 17,000 in 2025, and 21,627 in 2026.

On the contrary, the number of funded proposals has remained relatively static, fluctuating generally between 1,200 and 1,700 throughout the entire 13-year period. The system's capacity to fund projects has not expanded to meet the new demand.

3. Chance of Funding (%)

Analysis:

The success rate (chance of funding) illustrates the squeeze caused by the increase in applications against a relatively fixed number of awards. Historically, the success rate has been competitive but relatively stable, averaging around 14-18% between 2014 and 2024.

However, the dramatic increase in proposals in 2025 and 2026 has caused the success rate to plummet. It dropped to a single-digit 9.56% in 2025 and further down to an all-time low of 7.40% in 2026.

Conclusion & Outlook

The data reveals a highly competitive landscape that is rapidly intensifying. While the absolute budget for MSCA Fellowships has grown in recent years, the exponential rise in applicant volume has severely depressed the success rates.

  • Hyper-Competition: The drop from a ~16% success rate in 2024 to ~7.4% in 2026 indicates that obtaining an MSCA fellowship has become vastly more difficult.
  • Budget vs. Demand Mismatch: The budget increases have not been nearly sufficient to accommodate the flood of new applications.
  • External Factors: The 2024 surge in both budget and proposal numbers correlates with the United Kingdom officially joining Horizon Europe, which expanded the financial pool and reopened a historically popular destination for applicants. Concurrently, the widespread availability of Generative AI/LLMs may have lowered the barrier to writing and submitting proposals, further contributing to the dramatic and continued increase in volume up to 2026.
  • Strategic Implications for Researchers: Prospective applicants must recognize this new reality. The quality of proposals must be exceptionally high to stand out in a pool of over 21,000 candidates where fewer than 1 in 13 will be funded.
